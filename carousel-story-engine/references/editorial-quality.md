# Editorial quality gate

Use this reference for every full copy deck and carousel audit.

## Contents

## 1. Build a claim ledger

Classify every material claim before drafting:

| Class | Meaning | Allowed treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Sourced fact | Directly supported by a named source | Publish a compact adjacent citation and retain the full verification note |
| Supplied observation | Present in the user's case, transcript, or dataset | Attribute to the supplied material |
| Derived calculation | Computed only from supplied or sourced values | Show the inputs, time window, and sensible rounding |
| Thesis | Reasoned interpretation without empirical proof | Use calibrated language and allow disagreement |
| Hypothetical | Invented only to explain or demonstrate | Label as illustrative; never merge with evidence |
| Recommendation | Editorial or operational advice | Explain the reasoning or condition behind it |

Reject any sentence whose class is unclear. Never upgrade a plausible detail into an observation.

For legal, medical, financial, regulatory, or other consequential topics:

- verify the effective date and jurisdiction;
- distinguish enacted rules, guidance, proposals, and editorial recommendations;
- prefer primary sources;
- state the information cutoff;
- keep professional-review boundaries visible where they affect action.

For each material factual slide, define both:

- **public citation:** short source name or keyed marker visible on the slide;
- **verification note:** full title, URL, date, and claim mapping in production notes.

Put full source details in the caption, a source panel, or accessible end matter. Never leave the citation only in hidden production notes.

Do not combine a full bibliography with substantive content or the CTA when it makes either hard to scan. Keep compact markers in the deck and move URLs to the caption unless a dedicated source slide fits the requested page count.

## 2. Map the promise

Write an internal promise map:

```text
Cover claim:
Specific promises or counts:
Slide that fulfills each promise:
Evidence supporting each promise:
Reader action enabled at the end:
```

Remove any promise without a destination. Make every numbered promise match the exact number of visible, distinct items.

## 3. Protect the source boundary

- Preserve the subject, scope, denominator, time window, and causal limits of every observation.
- Preserve the exact measured unit in headlines and data cards. Do not turn tables into people, reserved units into reservations, tickets into customers, percentages into percentage points, or an informal estimate into a precise count.
- Do not infer conversion, revenue, intent, sentiment, demographics, or causality from a proxy metric.
- Use causal verbs only when the source supports causation. For before-and-after observations, describe the sequence and result without claiming what caused the change.
- Label reconstructed screens, conversations, examples, and workflows as illustrative.
- Keep recommendations visually and verbally separate from measured results.
- When a source lacks detail, keep the detail absent instead of completing the story creatively.

## 4. Edit for a native human voice

- Write in the requested language rather than translating an English draft.
- Preserve natural articles, prepositions, connectors, and idioms.
- Read every slide aloud; repair clipped, ceremonial, or overly polished phrasing.
- Vary sentence length and headline construction without manufacturing drama.
- Rewrite three consecutive fragments or sentences with the same grammatical shape. Keep parallel wording only inside a functional checklist, comparison, or data display where scanability is the purpose.
- Rewrite paired closing sentences that manufacture contrast through symmetry instead of explaining a difference.
- Prefer the vocabulary of the audience over generic marketing language.
- Keep one sharp observation when removing it would make the deck interchangeable with another account.

## 5. Control narrative rhythm

- Give every slide one identifiable contribution: context, tension, mechanism, proof, consequence, example, decision, or action.
- Write a three-to-seven-word internal contribution label for each slide. Merge or rewrite duplicate labels before drafting copy.
- Do not stack two abstract slides.
- Do not place two checklists, question grids, or advice lists consecutively.
- Follow dense explanation with a lighter proof, artifact, comparison, or image-led pause when possible.
- Make the final slide add a decision, diagnostic, reframe, or next step. Never use it only to summarize.
- Avoid repeating the same headline grammar or primary visual composition more than twice in succession.

## 6. Make conversion honest

For a lead or sales objective, specify:

```text
Offer: what the reader receives
Fit: who benefits and under what condition
Mechanism: keyword, URL, form, message, or booking path
Expectation: what happens after the action
Boundary: what is not promised
```

Repeat a keyword or URL in the caption when necessary for usability, but add information beyond the final slide.

Do not invent the mechanism. Ask for the real route or insert a bracketed production placeholder. Never publish a fabricated link-in-bio instruction, keyword, price, deadline, capacity, or deliverable.

## 7. Keep production notes executable

- Match each component to the information it carries.
- Specify placement, scale, hierarchy, density, accent behavior, and required assets.
- Name every asset as supplied, sourced, generated, reconstructed, or unnecessary. Provide a no-asset fallback for any optional photo.
- Keep the declared palette and component system consistent across all slides.
- Prefer annotated source artifacts and literal diagrams over decorative metaphors.
- Mark illustrative screens, messages, or documents so they cannot be mistaken for evidence.
- Check that text volume fits the proposed composition at mobile size.
- After rendering, inspect a contact sheet and the densest slides at original resolution. Reject clipping, overflow, missing fonts, broken assets, weak contrast, unsafe margins, or inconsistent components.
- Remove swipe instructions and arrows from the final artwork; the platform already supplies the interaction.

## 8. Apply the fail-fast gate

Reject and revise the deck if any answer is yes:

- Did any fact, detail, quotation, category, source, or result get invented?
- Did a thesis, recommendation, or hypothetical become a measured claim?
- Did a count, date, label, or source conflict elsewhere in the deck?
- Did the cover promise more than the slides deliver?
- Could two slides be merged without losing information?
- Does the caption merely retell the deck?
- Does the CTA ask for engagement without a relevant reason or delivery path?
- Does any visual require the designer to invent the informational structure?
- Does the copy sound translated, formulaic, or interchangeable with another account?
- Does the selected platform receive the wrong file type, aspect ratio, hashtag behavior, or caption convention?
- Did source visibility make any slide too dense for the stated mobile type sizes?
- Did a requested production brief or JSON omit any required slide field instead of marking it `none`?

Deliver only after every answer is no.
